04 - The agenda

The two
days

Roughly 60% hands-on. Prepared datasets on day one so nobody is blocked, your own data on day two.

Day one - foundations

Getting agents to do real work on spatial data

1. The mindset shift

An agent with a terminal, not a coding tool. It drives the software you already run. “Here's a folder of LAZ files, ground-classify them and give me a quality report.”

2. Data wrangling and conversion

Batch conversion across E57, LAS/LAZ, COPC, Shapefile, GPKG, GeoParquet. Reprojection, tiling, merging, repairing broken CRS metadata. The daily grind, considerably faster.

3. Point cloud pre-processing

PDAL pipelines described in plain language: denoising, thinning, clipping to boundaries, ground classification, DEM and DSM generation. The agent writes the pipeline, runs it, inspects the result.

Day two - your data

From one-off to something you keep using

4. Analysis and reporting

Volume calculations, change detection, zonal statistics, plots, a formatted report at the end. Dataset to deliverable in one session.

5. Your first agent

Turning a workflow into a reusable skill or script. Running it headless on a schedule or a watch folder, with checkpoints and validation.

6. Trust

Verifying results, sanity checks, visual QA, and where agents silently get geo wrong. The module that decides whether any of this reaches a client deliverable.

Plus

The compliance questions you'll be asked internally anyway: data privacy, on-premise and EU hosting, what to tell your public sector clients.

Questions people ask first

What does the team need beforehand?

Laptops they have admin rights on, and a Claude subscription per participant. I send exact setup instructions two weeks ahead and help if anything fights back. Nobody needs to install anything clever.

Does anyone need to be able to program?

No. It helps if some of them have written a script or a model builder chain at some point, because they will recognise what the agent is doing. It is not a requirement.

On-site or remote?

Both work, same price. On-site is better if the group is 5 or 6 people. Remote is fine for 3 or 4 and saves everyone a hotel.

What happens after the two days?

90 days of async questions, included. If the team wants more, there is a follow-up day or ongoing support - discussed then, not sold now.

Is this going to be out of date in six months?

The specific commands, partly - the tools move fast. The way of working, no. And that is what the two days are actually about.
