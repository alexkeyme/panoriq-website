01 - What agents actually do

Not a chatbot.
Not a detector.

"AI on our data" usually means one of two things to people. Neither is what I build.

What people expect

A chatbot that talks about your data

Ask a question, get a summary. Useful for a quick answer. It never touches the workflow, and the hours stay exactly where they were.

What people expect

A model that detects or classifies

One step of the pipeline, done a bit better or a bit worse. Those models improve slowly, and I don't promise a better classifier. Everything around that step still has to be done by hand.

What I build

An agent that runs the workflow

It opens your tools, runs the steps, checks the results, re-runs what failed and assembles the deliverable - unattended, for minutes or hours - then brings you the result and the evidence. Work on the data, not chat about it.

Nobody believes this from a paragraph. Everybody gets it from watching one run.

03 - Reliability

Reliable by design,
not by luck.

A demo proves an agent can do the job once. Your team needs it to do the job on Tuesday, on the ugly dataset, without anyone watching. That is engineering work, and it is most of what I do.

Ⅰ

Success is defined with your experts

Before an agent runs anything, we write down what a correct result looks like - in your team's terms, on your data. Their judgment becomes the yardstick.

Ⅱ

Every run is measured

Test sets and repeated runs show where the agent is right, where it fails, and how often. Numbers your team can check, not a demo reel.

Ⅲ

Checkpoints where they belong

Steps with real consequences wait for a person. The agent brings the result and the evidence; your team decides. Autonomy is earned per step, not assumed.

Agents fail in ways that look confident. The work is finding those failures before your clients do.
